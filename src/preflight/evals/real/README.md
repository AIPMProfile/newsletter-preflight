# Mail people actually sent

The sample emails this product is scored against are generated from the checks
that score them. That has a hard consequence: **they cannot contain a failure
nobody thought of.** A perfect result there means the checks agree with
themselves.

This folder is where that circularity breaks. Real broadcasts, from real
senders, written without any knowledge of what we look for.

## What counts as evidence

Not everything that looks like a newsletter is one.

**Sent mail** — an export from a sending platform, a message saved out of an
inbox, or a "view this in your browser" page that serves the message as it went
out. **Only these support any claim about email.**

**Archive pages** — a publisher's web version of a newsletter. These are web
pages. Their styling is produced by a website, not by the message that landed in
anyone's inbox, and they behave differently in the exact way that matters most:
across the archive pages sampled so far, *none* collapsed on a dark screen,
because web pages paint their own background and email templates frequently do
not. They are reported separately and never folded into a headline.

A number built from the second category, presented as though it were the first,
would be confidently wrong about the single most common failure this product
exists to catch.

## Before adding anything

Strip recipient names, addresses, and anything that identifies a subscriber.
Replace personalisation with placeholder tags. Nothing here should be able to
tell you who received it — which is also why the mail itself is not committed,
only the record of where it came from.

Expect the results here to be worse than the generated samples. That gap is the
finding: it is the distance between passing the checks we wrote and working on
the mail people send.
